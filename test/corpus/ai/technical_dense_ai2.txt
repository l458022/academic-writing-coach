Revenue recognition under IFRS 15 requires entities to identify distinct performance obligations within customer contracts and allocate the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices. The standard establishes a five-step framework applicable across industries. Variable consideration must be estimated using either the expected value or most likely amount method, constrained to amounts for which a significant reversal is improbable. Contract costs are capitalized when they relate directly to a contract, generate resources used to satisfy performance obligations, and are expected to be recovered. Deferred tax implications arise when revenue recognition timing diverges between financial reporting and tax bases, creating temporary differences that require recognition of deferred tax assets or liabilities. Measurement of deferred tax balances requires assessment of expected reversal patterns and applicable tax rates. Entities must evaluate recoverability of deferred tax assets considering projected taxable income and tax planning strategies. Disclosure requirements mandate reconciliation of expected to effective tax rates and details of unrecognized deferred tax liabilities. Lease accounting under IFRS 16 similarly creates deferred tax effects through temporary differences between right-of-use assets and lease liabilities.
