This study investigates the impact of digital transformation on organizational performance in emerging markets. Using a mixed-methods approach combining quantitative survey data from 342 firms and qualitative interviews with 28 executives, we examine how technology adoption mediates the relationship between strategic orientation and firm outcomes. The findings indicate that digital capability significantly moderates this relationship, suggesting that firms with higher technological readiness achieve superior performance outcomes. Furthermore, our analysis reveals that organizational culture plays a critical mediating role in translating digital investments into tangible results. These insights contribute to the existing literature by providing empirical evidence on the mechanisms through which digital transformation creates value. The study offers practical implications for managers seeking to navigate technological change in uncertain environments. Consistent with prior research, we find that firms operating in competitive markets demonstrate greater sensitivity to technological shifts. Our results also suggest that the relationship between digital investment and performance is nonlinear, with diminishing returns observed beyond certain thresholds. These findings underscore the importance of strategic alignment between technology initiatives and broader organizational objectives. The implications for theory and practice are discussed, along with limitations and directions for future research.
