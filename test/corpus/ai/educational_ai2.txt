Supply and demand form the foundation of market economics. When demand for a product increases while supply remains constant, prices tend to rise. Conversely, when supply exceeds demand, prices typically fall. This relationship creates an equilibrium point where the quantity consumers wish to purchase equals the quantity producers wish to sell. Several factors influence demand, including consumer preferences, income levels, and prices of substitutes. Supply is affected by production costs, technology, and expectations about future prices. Understanding these dynamics helps explain price movements observed in real markets. Government interventions such as price controls can create shortages or surpluses by preventing markets from reaching equilibrium. Elasticity measures how responsive quantity is to changes in price, and this concept is essential for predicting the effects of various policies. Markets characterized by high elasticity respond strongly to price changes, whereas inelastic markets exhibit muted responses. These principles apply across diverse contexts, from agricultural commodities to labor markets.
