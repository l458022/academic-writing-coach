Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose molecules. This process occurs primarily in the chloroplasts, which contain the pigment chlorophyll responsible for absorbing light. The overall reaction requires carbon dioxide and water as inputs, producing glucose and oxygen as outputs. Photosynthesis proceeds in two distinct stages: the light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle. During the light reactions, solar energy is captured and converted into ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle then uses these energy carriers to fix carbon dioxide into organic molecules. Understanding this process is fundamental to comprehending how energy flows through ecosystems. The efficiency of photosynthesis varies considerably depending on environmental conditions such as light intensity, temperature, and carbon dioxide concentration. Plants employ various adaptations to optimize photosynthetic performance in different habitats. C4 and CAM pathways represent specialized mechanisms that reduce photorespiration losses in hot and arid environments.
