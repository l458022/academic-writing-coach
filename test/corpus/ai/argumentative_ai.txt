Climate change represents one of the most pressing challenges facing contemporary society. The scientific consensus regarding anthropogenic contributions to global warming is well established, yet policy responses remain inadequate. Several factors contribute to this gap between knowledge and action. First, the dispersed nature of both causes and consequences complicates collective action. Second, economic interests frequently conflict with environmental imperatives. Third, political cycles incentivize short-term thinking over long-term planning. Addressing this challenge requires coordinated international effort, technological innovation, and significant behavioral change. While the scale of the problem is daunting, emerging renewable technologies and evolving social norms offer grounds for cautious optimism. Mitigation strategies must be complemented by adaptation measures, particularly in vulnerable regions. Equitable burden-sharing arrangements are essential for sustaining international cooperation over time.
