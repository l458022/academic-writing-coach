Contemporary life is frequently characterised by a sense of relentless activity. It is important to examine what this phenomenon reveals about modern priorities. Firstly, the widespread availability of communication tools means that work increasingly intrudes upon personal time. Secondly, competitive pressures in education and employment encourage individuals to accumulate commitments. Moreover, social comparison may lead people to equate a full calendar with personal worth. However, this pattern is not without cost. Furthermore, research consistently associates chronic overcommitment with elevated stress and reduced cognitive performance. It is also apparent that periods of apparent inactivity often produce valuable insight. In conclusion, reclaiming space for rest and reflection may prove more beneficial than striving to remain constantly occupied.
