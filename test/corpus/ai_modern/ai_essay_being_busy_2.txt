The value that societies place on being busy has attracted increasing scrutiny. This essay argues that busyness is not intrinsically virtuous. To begin with, constant activity frequently crowds out reflection, which is essential for sound judgement. Furthermore, individuals who are perpetually occupied may struggle to recognise when their efforts are misdirected. In addition, the cultural association between busyness and importance can obscure the reality that much of the work performed is routine rather than consequential. Nevertheless, it must be acknowledged that for many people, busyness reflects genuine economic necessity rather than personal preference. Consequently, judgements about how others allocate their time should be made cautiously. In conclusion, the question worth asking is not whether one is busy, but whether one's activity is purposeful.
